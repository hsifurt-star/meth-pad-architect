# Evidence note: responsible scope for a multi-substance calculator

## WHO ASSIST

WHO describes ASSIST as a tool developed to help identify substance-use-related health risks and substance-use disorders in primary and general healthcare, to support brief intervention or referral. Its score is a substance-specific screening result, not a probability of addiction, brain damage, lifespan loss, or an individual prognosis. The manual describes clinical administration, scoring, and interpretation. [1]

## NIDA TAPS

TAPS is a two-stage screening and brief-assessment instrument. Stage 1 asks frequency over the past 12 months; the brief assessment asks about substance-specific problems over the past three months. NIDA lists score categories as 0 (no use in the past three months), 1 (problem use), and 2+ (higher risk). NIDA cautions that the recommended 2+ cutoff has adequate sensitivity above 70% for DSM-5 SUD only for tobacco, alcohol, and marijuana; other substances need further assessment from a score of 1+. NIDA says the tool is for use under medical-provider supervision and is not intended to guide self-diagnosis or treatment decisions. [2]

A validation study in 1,523 adult patients filling opioid prescriptions at 19 pharmacies in Ohio and Indiana compared TAPS against ASSIST risk categories. Discrimination varied by substance and risk threshold. This narrow sample does not validate a model that combines arbitrary drug lists, doses, routes, age, and years of use into a personal probability. [3]

## Implementation implication

The website can present faithful screening scores or categories only where the validated instrument supports the exact substances, questions, scoring, population, and intended setting. It should label them as screening—not diagnosis or probability. Age, duration, dose, route, and multiple-substance history may be retained only as optional local context if useful, and must not change a numeric score unless a cited, validated model explicitly uses those inputs. For unvalidated combinations, report “not estimable from available validated models” rather than inventing a number. Dose and route must never produce safe-use instructions or a personalized guarantee.
